REAL HISTORY IN THIS VIDEO
• Most of Rome's population lived in rented flats in multi-storey insulae
• Augustus limited the height of buildings in Rome to 70 Roman feet (about 20 m)
• Upper floors of insulae were the cheapest and most dangerous; ground floors held shops and better flats
• Augustus established the vigiles in AD 6: seven cohorts of firefighters/night watchmen
• Romans divided daylight into 12 hours whose length varied with the season (~45-75 modern minutes)
• Most insula residents fetched water from public fountains
• Ancient Rome was supplied by eleven aqueducts by the 3rd century AD
• Fullers (laundries) used collected urine for cleaning cloth
• Vespasian taxed urine collection; 'pecunia non olet' is attributed to him
• The toga was heavy formal wear; everyday clothing was the tunic
• The Roman breakfast (ientaculum) was light, often bread with cheese or fruit, or skipped
• Carbonised loaves scored into eight segments were found at Pompeii

Stan's story is a dramatization; the historical facts are real, simplified for the video, and sourced below. Numbers are estimates — historians argue about the details, and that's part of the fun.
