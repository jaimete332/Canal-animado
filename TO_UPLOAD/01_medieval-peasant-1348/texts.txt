REAL HISTORY IN THIS VIDEO
• England's civil/legal year began on 25 March until 1752
• The Black Death reached England in 1348
• Peasant houses were timber-framed with wattle-and-daub walls and thatched roofs
• Most peasant houses had an open central hearth and no chimney
• In some regions peasants lived in longhouses shared with livestock
• Villeins were unfree tenants legally bound to their lord's manor
• Villeins typically owed 'week-work' on the lord's demesne, often 2-3 days per week
• The lord's demesne was farmed using villeins' labor services
• The reeve was a villager chosen to oversee work on the manor
• Merchet was a fine paid to the lord when an unfree woman married
• Heriot: on a tenant's death the lord took the best beast
• Tenants were often obliged to use the lord's mill and pay a toll (multure) in grain

Stan's story is a dramatization; the historical facts are real, simplified for the video, and sourced below. Numbers are estimates — historians argue about the details, and that's part of the fun.
